# What adaptive control teaches about test-time memory

*Draft, 2026-10-24. A research note beside [fast-memory-design.md](fast-memory-design.md) (designs A–C) and [#11](https://github.com/alinaos-org/engram/issues/11) (write trigger). Every claim about the literature links to its source. Mapping those results onto Engram is our own reading. Every proposal in this note is **untested**, including the "What it suggests (untested)" column of Section 2, and the Section 3 labels (AC1–AC9) are separate from the Phase 1 hypotheses H1–H5 in [phase-1-preregistration.md](phase-1-preregistration.md).*

## 1. The related problem

Engram aims to store each user's memory in the network itself: a frozen base model plus a fast neural memory written at inference time. The candidate designs in [fast-memory-design.md](fast-memory-design.md) write it by a closed-form ridge solve (no backprop) into in-place MLP weight deltas ΔW (Design A), by one gradient step per chunk with Titans-style momentum and learned decay on a 3-layer MLP side memory (Design B), or by a sparse gradient step on slots (Design C). They write on every chunk or turn, with surprise gating left as an open ablation ([#11](https://github.com/alinaos-org/engram/issues/11)); only Design C would use surprise, and only to set how many slots t a write touches. A linear memory appears only as a depth ablation ([experiment-plan.md](experiment-plan.md), Phase 1). Periodic offline "sleep" consolidation is planned to move what the fast memory holds into a per-user LoRA adapter, and a retrieval log keeps the source turns. A central open risk ([open-questions.md](open-questions.md), OQ7 and OQ9), after the question of whether a frozen-base memory can store facts usefully at all (OQ1), is that these online writes destabilise or drift. The failure modes the repo names are drift from repetitive self-training (OQ7), writes on irrelevant spans (OQ6, [#11](https://github.com/alinaos-org/engram/issues/11)), collapse over many sequential edits or sleeps (OQ9), and interference with general ability ([eval-design.md](eval-design.md), category 10). Unbounded parameter growth is our addition.

For about seven decades, adaptive control has worked on a closely related problem: adjusting parameters online, inside a feedback loop, without losing stability ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). Early adaptive flight control was built and flown without analysis. Åström's history of that era lists disasters in flight tests, among them the X-15 crash of November 1967, but the slides do not state its cause ([Åström 2018](https://lccc.control.lth.se/media/LCCC2018/WS2018-10/Slides/KJA.pdf)). The gradient-based MIT rule turned out to be capable of causing instability, especially when there is phase lag between measuring the error and adjusting the parameters ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). Lyapunov's method was then proposed in place of gradient descent and became the foundation for the stability of adaptive systems ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). By 1966 Parks and others had redesigned the MIT-rule laws with Lyapunov's method, which set the stage for rigorous stability proofs in adaptive control ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control), Sec. 1.3). Ioannou & Sun list Lyapunov design alongside gradient and least-squares laws on an estimation-error cost as the stable replacements for the MIT rule (Sec. 1.2.6). The survey reports that Rohrs et al. (1982) then showed both gradient and Lyapunov laws to be inadequate for robustness to bounded disturbances and unmodelled dynamics ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). Rohrs's own thesis found an infinite-gain operator in every algorithm it examined ([Rohrs 1982](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19830005594)). The field did not give up on adaptation. It added robust modifications: leakage, projection, dead zones, normalisation and excitation monitoring ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control); [Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/)). Run-time assurance separates safety enforcement from performance goals by filtering the output of an unverified primary controller ([Hobbs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03506)).

This note treats one design choice as open: whether Engram writes on the tokens it generates itself. If it does, the memory shapes the generated text and that text becomes the memory's next input. The write rule then sits in a feedback loop and can be analysed like an adaptive law. Writing only on user and prompt tokens opens that loop. Even a closed loop does not have MRAC's structure, because no plant with its own dynamics sits between the parameters and the error. The lesson of this history is to analyse whatever feedback path exists explicitly. A per-token update that is well behaved in isolation should not be assumed to stay well behaved inside that loop.

## 2. Mapping adaptive-control concepts to Engram

| Adaptive-control concept | Engram counterpart | What it suggests (untested) |
|---|---|---|
| **MIT rule vs Lyapunov design.** The MIT rule θ̇ = −k e ∇θe can destabilise ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). It puts online estimates in place of the unknown parameters inside the sensitivity functions. In general only local stability can be shown, for a small gain, a small reference with enough frequencies, and initial estimates near the truth ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). Lyapunov design takes θ̇ = −k(t) e φ and chooses k and φ from a Lyapunov function. For the full-state MRAC law, the update can be written as a gradient step on a loss that adds a term reflecting the system dynamics (Eqs. 45–46, after Gaudio et al.). In the general output-feedback case, the survey says the error model does not allow such simple loss functions, and in general φ need not be the gradient of any loss ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). [Åström 2018](https://lccc.control.lth.se/media/LCCC2018/WS2018-10/Slides/KJA.pdf) works an example. | Surprise-gradient write to the fast memory | For a linear delta-rule memory, the write is an exact SGD step on a static per-token regression loss ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484)). The MIT rule's failure, approximate sensitivities passed through plant dynamics, therefore does not carry over directly. It applies where the error depends dynamically on the memory, for example through writes on generated text or coupling across layers. That feedback path is the open analysis problem. |
| **Time-scale separation and gain.** The MIT rule fails when the adaptation time scale collides with the plant's ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). For slowly varying parameters, the recommended ordering is parameter variation < adaptation speed < model-reference bandwidth < reference bandwidth < unmodelled dynamics ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/)). A large γr² excites parasitic dynamics and gives unbounded solutions ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). In standard MRAC, a large adaptive gain produces high-gain control behaviour: high-frequency oscillations that can excite unmodelled dynamics and lead to instability. Nguyen et al. propose an optimal control modification meant to allow large gains without these effects ([Nguyen et al. 2008](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090026330)). For convex costs on a bounded set, the fixed-step dynamic-regret bound has terms in L/η and Tη, where L is the comparator's path length ([Zinkevich 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~maz/publications/techconvex.pdf)). The step that minimises it grows with L. | Write rate vs token-level dynamics, user drift and sleep | This is a tension, not a rule. A fast memory binds an item in one write: with unit-norm keys and β = 1, the transition erases along k before the new value is written ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484)). Apply the ordering to the memory's aggregate drift rate, not to the per-item binding step, and run consolidation on the slowest clock. Treat repetition loops as a possible time-scale collision. In the convex (linear-memory) case, faster user drift argues for a larger step. "Small fast step plus consolidation" is a hypothesis, not a consequence of the bound. |
| **LMS/NLMS step-size bound.** Under independent regressors and slow adaptation, mean (first-order) convergence needs 0 < μ < 2/λmax(R) for LMS and 0 < μ < 2 for NLMS. The NLMS bound uses a tap-delay-line approximation, the bounds are classical results cited from Sayed 2008, and mean-square stability is left to future work ([Lopes et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05746)). Take a fixed weight vector and exciting inputs. Then LMS with 0 < μ < inf_i 1/‖h_i‖² is the central H∞-optimal a priori filter, with energy gain exactly 1. Without exciting inputs it is only suboptimal ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)). DeltaNet's transition I − βkkᵀ has eigenvalues 1 (d − 1 times) and 1 − β‖k‖², and none may exceed one in magnitude ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484)). With unit-norm keys, a kkᵀ coefficient in (0,2) widens the eigenvalue range to [−1,1] and keeps the matrix norm at most 1 ([Grazzi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537)). | Delta-rule write strength β and key norm | Normalise keys, or clamp β‖k‖² wherever keys are not unit-norm. The mean bound is not a safety margin. The LMS H∞ result (Theorem 6) assumes effective steps μ‖h_i‖² below 1. The paper's first-principles proof (Appendix A) also allows exactly 1. Nothing in the paper covers steps in (1,2), and its scalar example at μ = 1.5 has an energy gain above 1 ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)). A non-expansive transition bounds only the homogeneous recursion, because the d − 1 unit eigenvalues remain. It does not bound the memory under conflicting or noisy writes, so projection or leakage is still needed. Without normalisation the safe LMS step depends on the key covariance, which moves across users and topics. |
| **Normalisation.** Take Goodwin's projection algorithm (normaliser 1 + φᵀφ), no noise, and a fixed θ*. Then the estimate's distance to θ* never exceeds its initial value ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). Longhorn's step δ/(1+δ‖k‖²) tends to 1/‖k‖² as δ → ∞, which is NLMS, also called the projection or Kaczmarz method ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352)). In Schlag et al.'s WikiText-103 ablation of linear Transformers trained with their delta update rule (Sec. 6.3, Table 3), every run used sum normalisation, which divides φ(k) and φ(q) by the sum of their components, because the models diverged without it ([Schlag et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)). That is a different normaliser from 1 + ‖k‖². In DeltaNet's 340M-parameter ablation (15B tokens), switching the query and key normalisation from L1 to L2 greatly improved performance, e.g. Wikitext perplexity 31.12 → 28.03 with the 1+ELU feature map ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484)). | Normalised write step | Make a normalised write the default, and say which one. Goodwin divides by 1 + ‖k‖², which is a constant 1/2 once keys are unit-norm. NLMS divides by ‖k‖². DeltaNet uses unit-norm keys times β. Their guarantees differ. The noise-free guarantee assumes a fixed realisable target, which Engram lacks (Section 4), so the baseline is well understood rather than known to be safe. Schlag et al.'s divergence is evidence about training stability, not about writes at test time. |
| **Model order and capacity.** Use no more parameters than the modelling needs ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). Higher order needs more excitation and causes ill-conditioning ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/)). A d-dimensional key space holds at most d orthogonal keys, so more associations than that interfere. The delta rule corrects the stored value instead of adding to it ([Schlag et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11174)). | Fast-memory size; catastrophic interference | Prefer corrective (delta) writes over additive (Hebbian) ones. Size is a trade-off. A smaller memory has fewer unexcited directions to drift in, but it reaches the orthogonal-key limit sooner and interferes more. Leave the size as an empirical choice. |
| **σ-modification (leakage).** The term −γσθ bounds θ. Even with no disturbance it can leave a nonzero steady-state error ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). It coincides with ℓ2 regularisation ([Gaudio et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05856)). Gaudio et al. cite Ioannou & Kokotovic 1984 for σ-modification. Under parasitic dynamics, that paper guarantees only a region of attraction from which signals converge to a residual set ([Ioannou & Kokotovic 1984](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1984.1103490)). Gated DeltaNet is a reparameterisation of leaky LMS that ties decay to step size ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352)). In the paper's S-NIAH case study (§3.2, Table 2), DeltaNet (no decay) stays near-perfect on S-NIAH-1 (passkey retrieval in repeated synthetic context). On S-NIAH-2/3 (needle in a real-essay haystack) it falls sharply from 2K tokens on. The authors attribute this to poor memory clearance, which causes memory collision in a fixed-size state. Mamba2 (uniform decay) degrades beyond 2K tokens because it decays history too quickly ([Yang, Kautz & Hatamizadeh 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06464)). | Learned decay | Decay buys boundedness at the price of forgetting clean memories. Whether decay should be input-gated is a hypothesis to test. Decouple decay from the write step; [Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352) suggest this but did not test it. |
| **Shifted and switching leakage.** The term −w(θ − θ₀) pulls toward a prior estimate. Switching-σ leaks only when the parameter norm exceeds M₀, and it keeps the ideal properties if M₀ exceeds the true parameter norm ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). | Decay target; memory-norm budget | Test decay toward the last consolidated state, not toward zero, applied only above a norm budget (AC3). Engram has no θ* to check M₀ against, so set the budget empirically. |
| **e-modification.** Leakage scaled by the size of the output error ([Narendra & Annaswamy 1987](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1987.1104543); [Gaudio et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05856)) keeps solutions bounded without persistent excitation (PE). With a persistently exciting reference input of sufficiently large amplitude, the origin of the error equations is exponentially stable ([Narendra & Annaswamy 1987](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1987.1104543)). Sastry & Bodson say e-modification may be less prone to bursting than σ-modification, but that this has not been fully substantiated ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/), Sec. 5.7.2). | Surprise-scaled decay | This decay is global. It switches off when current surprise is low. When surprise is high, for example at a topic switch, it erodes all memories, including unrelated, well-predicted ones. To protect well-predicted memories, decay per direction or per slot instead. |
| **Dead zone.** Stop adapting while the error is below a disturbance bound ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control); [Gaudio et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05856)). The dead zone needs an a priori upper bound on the modelling error; if the bound is underestimated, the guarantees no longer hold ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control), Sec. 8.4.3). Sastry & Bodson suggest setting the threshold from the observed noise floor, and a relative (normalised) dead zone is claimed to avoid bursting ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/), Sec. 5.7.1). For MRAC, the dead zone and projection are called essential for predictable operation ([Lavretsky & Wise 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4471-4396-3)). | Surprise gate | A dead zone guards against low-amplitude noise. Irrelevant text need not be low-surprise, so blocking salient irrelevant input needs a separate relevance gate. Set the threshold from the measured surprise noise floor, check that it stays below the surprise of relevant facts, and use normalised surprise. Whether Engram needs the gate as much as MRAC does is a hypothesis. |
| **Projection operator.** In continuous time the operator is continuous. It leaves the update unchanged where f(θ) ≤ 0, or when the update points inward, and it scales back the outward component in the band 0 < f ≤ 1. If f(θ₀) ≤ 1, then f(θ(t)) ≤ 1 for all t (Lemma 9). The column-wise version keeps each column norm at most v + ε when the column starts inside the norm-v ball (Example 10) ([Lavretsky & Gibson 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4232)). Projection onto a convex set keeps every property of the unconstrained law ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)), provided θ* lies in the set. For the smooth operator, Lemma 6 of [Lavretsky & Gibson 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4232) needs θ* in the inner set Ω0 = {f ≤ 0}, not merely in Ω1 = {f ≤ 1}. Under that condition it gives (θ − θ*)ᵀ(Proj(θ, y, f) − y) ≤ 0, so projection cannot increase the usual Lyapunov function. Regret bounds for discrete projected steps assume a bounded set and bounded gradients ([Zinkevich 2003](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~maz/publications/techconvex.pdf)). | Norm ball on fast-memory columns or heads, and on the LoRA delta | Lemma 9 is a continuous-time result, and a discrete write can jump out of the set. For discrete writes, compare a projected step (for a norm ball, rescaling onto the ball) with smooth damping in the boundary layer. Size the budget to hold the memories that are needed; there is no θ* to check it against. The Γ-projection variant (Definition 11, with Lemma 12 giving the matching inequality) uses a fixed symmetric positive-definite gain Γ, so it fits writes with a constant preconditioner. The paper gives no result for a time-varying or data-dependent preconditioner, and states no Lemma-9-style invariance result for Γ-projection ([Lavretsky & Gibson 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4232)). |
| **Persistent excitation (PE).** In model reference adaptive control with a bounded reference input that has an autocovariance, the parameter error converges to zero exponentially if and only if the reference's spectral measure is supported on at least 2n points, where 2n is the number of adjustable parameters. When the spectrum has only k < 2n lines, the error need not converge to zero. Instead, the estimate's distance to a (2n − k)-dimensional affine set goes to zero: the set of parameters for which the closed loop matches the model at those frequencies. The estimate need not converge to any point in that set ([Boyd & Sastry 1986](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/para_conv_adaptive.html)). Without PE and with a bounded disturbance, the error can vanish while θ grows like (1+t)^(1/4) ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). Under closed-loop set-point control, estimates can drift until the loop goes unstable. The instability enriches the signals, the loop recovers, and the cycle repeats. This is bursting ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). Remedies include monitoring excitation and switching adaptation off when it is poor, or injecting perturbation signals ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/); [Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). | Long monotone or repetitive sessions | Treat the memory as identified only along key directions that have been excited. Do not freeze writes just because the key covariance is poorly conditioned. Any window shorter than d_k tokens is rank-deficient, and a rank-1 delta write leaves the memory's response orthogonal to k unchanged. Track a statistic tied to the actual risk, such as norm growth from conflicting writes along nearly collinear keys. Bursting needs a closed loop that Engram may not have, so it is a hypothesis for Engram, not a predicted mechanism. |
| **RLS forgetting and covariance blow-up.** Without PE, a forgetting factor in (0,1) makes some singular values of P_k diverge. In finite precision this can make the estimate diverge (Example 9). Variable-direction forgetting applies forgetting only in the information-rich subspace. In the paper's examples without PE (Examples 11–12), it kept every singular value of P_k bounded. An upper bound on P_k is proven only when PE holds (Proposition 10) ([Goel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03523)). Ioannou & Sun use "covariance wind-up" for the opposite effect: pure least squares goes numb as P shrinks. They also note that a forgetting factor can let P blow up unless it is capped ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). Gaudio et al.'s adaptive-gain law (18) has not been proven stable for the dynamic error model (7) (footnote 2). That law uses a regressor-normalised gain matrix with a forgetting factor and a cap ‖Γ‖ ≤ Γmax. The paper compares its regressor normalisation with the gradient-based normalisation of AdaGrad and Adam (Sec. 3.4) ([Gaudio et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.05856)). | Preconditioned or covariance-tracking write state; adaptive per-direction steps; momentum | Put explicit bounds on any per-direction gain state, and forget it only along the directions being written. A scalar decay on the memory state (Gated DeltaNet's α) has no P to blow up; it is leakage, covered in the σ-modification row. |
| **Concurrent learning.** Adapting on recorded and current data together gives exponential convergence of tracking and parameter error without PE. It needs only a checkable linear-independence condition on the recorded data ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2010.5717148)). For structured uncertainty the rate depends on the stored data through λ_min of Ω = Σ_j Φ(x_j)Φ(x_j)ᵀ, together with Q, P and Γ_W ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2010.5717148), Remark 4). The follow-up states that the rate is directly proportional to σ_min(Ω) and claims only ultimate boundedness for RBF-NN uncertainty ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2011.5991481)). In wing-rock simulations, the stack that maximised σ_min converged faster than static or cyclic stacks (final σ_min(Ω) 0.35, vs 0.10 for the cyclic stack and 0.03 for the static one) ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2011.5991481)). The method has been extended to all DNN layers. A smooth projection keeps the estimates in a compact set. Every update law also carries a σ-modification (leakage) term −γ2θ̂. If the excitation condition fails, this term helps keep the parameter error bounded, but a large γ2 enlarges the ultimate bounds and degrades tracking and estimation ([Hart et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10678)). | Retrieval log | Replay a few logged items with each write, and curate the log for coverage (σ_min), not recency. Track σ_min as a health signal once the log holds d_k independent keys; before that it is 0. The results assume a single fixed parameter generated all the data. Replace log entries on every update and deletion, because replaying a stale pair re-writes the old value (our inference: Chowdhary & Johnson 2010 assume a constant W* and never treat changing parameters or stale stored data). Their Remark 3 says only that adding or removing stored points leaves the Lyapunov function unchanged ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2010.5717148)). |
| **L1 adaptive control.** A low-pass filter on the adaptive control signal, together with a projection-bounded estimate, is claimed to let the adaptation gain be raised to tighten transient bounds on both the input and the output (the bounds shrink as 1/sqrt(Γ)) without high-frequency control. In a scalar example, the time-delay margin stays bounded away from zero as the gain grows, while MRAC's goes to zero ([Cao & Hovakimyan 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608320)). Later L1 work describes this as decoupling adaptation from robustness ([Hovakimyan et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCS.2011.941961)). A critique finds that the filter worsens performance and margin bounds relative to plain MRAC. It also finds that very high gains make the law stiff and less robust to unmodelled dynamics ([Ioannou et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2014.2318871)). | Filter or rate limit on the memory read-out | A read-out filter may smooth changes in behaviour. It complements a write bound rather than replacing one, and a very large discrete step still risks overshoot or instability. |
| **Simplex and run-time assurance.** Sha argues for using simplicity to control complexity ([Sha 2001](https://doi.org/10.1109/MS.2001.936213)). In Neural Simplex, a decision module hands control to a verified baseline when a proposed input would leave the baseline's recoverable region, which is defined through the plant dynamics. Switching back requires a stricter condition ([Phan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00528)). An RTA system has an input manager, a safety monitor, a switch and a recovery function ([ASTM F3269-21](https://store.astm.org/f3269-21.html)). Safety enforcement does not depend on how the primary controller is built ([Hobbs et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03506)). | Frozen base with memory read-out zeroed; last consolidated state | Use a simple, independently implemented heuristic monitor that hands output to the base when it trips, with hysteresis before writes are re-enabled. Simplex-style guarantees do not transfer without a formal safe set and a model of how memory state maps to behaviour. Classic Simplex treats the advanced controller as a black box and leaves the baseline in control, and Neural Simplex retrains its neural controller online while the baseline is in control instead of resetting it ([Phan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00528)). Resetting the memory to a consolidated state is our addition. |
| **Safe adaptive control and gated updates.** An abrupt controller change can destabilise the loop even when the new controller was designed from a good model. Safe adaptive control is one remedy, through small 'safe' changes or prospective checks before switching ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). Berkenkamp et al. certify a policy with a Lyapunov function, GP confidence bounds and Lipschitz constants. Outside their Algorithm 1, they remark that if the certified region shrinks, one can always revert to the previous, still-certified policy ([Berkenkamp et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08551)). Drift-plus-penalty queues raise the penalty on whichever past task's replay loss has degraded ([Shah et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08452)). | Sleep consolidation into the LoRA adapter | Validate a new adapter against replay and base-capability probes before swapping it in, and keep the previous one for rollback. This borrows the accept-or-revert pattern. It is empirical validation, not a certificate. |
| **Residual adaptation on a nominal model.** Offline networks invert a nominal model, and an online network learns the residual. Under mild assumptions all loop signals stay bounded ([Kim & Calise 1997](https://doi.org/10.2514/2.4029)). A frozen learned basis with damped, online-adapted linear coefficients gives an error-ball bound that grows with the damping term λ‖a‖ ([O'Connell et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06908)). | Fast memory over the frozen base | Scope writes to a correction on the base, and accept that decay adds a λ‖a‖ term to the guaranteed error bound in exchange for not needing persistent excitation. The source does not show that a larger λ loosens the bound overall, because its constants also depend on λ ([O'Connell et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.06908)). |
| **Momentum with normalised damping.** For linearly parameterised systems, the momentum adaptation laws are proven stable and convergent with time-varying damping N(t) = 1 + μ‖Y‖², where Y is the regressor and μ is above a threshold (μ > γ/(ηβ) in Prop. 5.1; Prop. 6.4 is the composite case). In the nonlinear monotone case the damping is N = 1 + μ‖α‖², where α is the known monotonicity direction (Prop. 6.5). Under the velocity-gradient convexity assumption, N(t) is any signal that "completes the square" (Assumptions 6.1/6.2), for example ‖∇â f̂‖². Suppose the estimate converges to a parameter vector that interpolates the dynamics along the trajectory. Then that limit is the interpolant minimising the Bregman divergence from the initial estimate. For the natural momentum laws the same holds, except that the divergence is measured from the initial auxiliary (momentum) variable v̂(0), not from â(0). This is stated formally only for the linearly parameterised case (Remark 8.3, Prop. G.1). These are continuous-time results ([Boffi & Slotine 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13154)). | Write momentum | Scale momentum damping by a squared gradient norm (for an MLP memory, the Jacobian norm), not by key norm, which is constant once keys are unit-norm. Centre the write geometry on the last checkpoint; for momentum writes, the anchor is the initial momentum variable. Discrete momentum with a data-dependent step is outside these results. |

## 3. Proposals for Engram (all untested)

Each item is a hypothesis for an exploratory engram-bench run ([phase-1-preregistration.md](phase-1-preregistration.md), "Not pre-registered"). AC1–AC3 and AC5 need session types that engram-bench does not plant yet: irrelevant and adversarial write streams, long repetitive sessions, topic switches, and topics revisited after long gaps. Unless an item says otherwise, the baseline is a plain normalised delta-rule (NLMS) memory with effective step β‖k‖² below 1. In that range, for a fixed weight vector, LMS keeps the energy of the predicted (a priori) errors at or below the energy of the disturbances: the initial weight error weighted by 1/μ, plus the observation noise. Exciting inputs make this gain of 1 the best any filter can do; without them, LMS still meets it but is suboptimal ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)). Engram has no fixed weight vector (Section 4), so the bound motivates the baseline but does not guarantee it. This baseline is a linear stand-in for designs A–C, none of which is implemented yet. The metrics are fact-recall accuracy (engram-bench category 1, "Fact recall", split direct vs indirect; at probe timing a for fast-memory arms, and b or c for AC8); update accuracy (FAMA-style: stale answers count as wrong), which is engram-bench category 4, "Fact updating"; forget compliance (category 7); and general-suite drift (change versus the bare base on the fixed MMLU-Pro and GSM8K subsets; engram-bench category 10, "Interference"). None of these experiments has been run.

- **AC1. Write envelope (untested).** Against an unnormalised, unclamped delta-rule memory, L2-normalised keys with β in (0,2) ([Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484); [Grazzi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.12537); with unit-norm keys this is also the NLMS step range for mean convergence under i.i.d. regressors and slow adaptation, [Lopes et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05746), eq. 41) will lower general-suite drift under irrelevant and adversarial streams, without lowering update accuracy. A second arm restricts β to (0,1], the range the LMS H∞ bound covers ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)), to measure what steps in (1,2) cost. Boffi & Slotine scale momentum damping by a squared regressor or gradient norm to prove continuous-time stability: N = 1 + μ‖Y‖², or N = ‖∇â f̂‖² in their example for Assumption 6.2 ([Boffi & Slotine 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13154)). Our untested hypothesis is that an analogous normalisation would further lower drift here. Falsified if drift is unchanged or update accuracy drops.
- **AC2. Excitation-aware writes (untested).** This arm makes three changes together:
  - a dead-zone threshold set from the measured surprise noise floor and kept below the surprise of relevant facts;
  - normalised surprise;
  - slower writes when the memory norm grows from conflicting writes along nearly collinear keys.

  The first two follow Sastry & Bodson's dead-zone guidance (Sec. 5.7.1: set the threshold from the observed noise floor; use a relative, normalised dead zone) ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/)). The third is our own substitute for their excitation monitoring (Sec. 5.6.1), not a method from the source. Its motivation is the bursting remedy of switching adaptation off when excitation is poor ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1)). Together they will lower general-suite drift after long repetitive sessions, while recall of facts stated in those sessions stays within noise of the ungated memory. A dead zone does not stop salient irrelevant input, so a relevance gate is tested as a separate arm.
- **AC3. Leakage ablation (untested).** Against a learned constant decay, three variants will give higher fact recall at matched drift: surprise-scaled decay ([Narendra & Annaswamy 1987](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1987.1104543)), the same decay applied per slot or per direction, and decay applied only above a norm budget ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)). Global surprise-scaled decay erodes all memories when surprise is high, so measure recall of unrelated memories after topic switches. Decoupling decay from the step size ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352)) will improve update accuracy. Decay toward the last consolidated state gets its own test, because it may hurt forget compliance if that state still holds an item the user deleted.
- **AC4. Projection plus norm monitoring (untested).** Two per-column variants will both bound fast-weight norms: projected writes, which rescale onto the norm ball after each step, and smooth boundary-layer damping modelled on [Lavretsky & Gibson 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.4232). The test is which one costs less fact recall. Lemma 9's bound is continuous-time and is not claimed for discrete writes. Log weight norms directly, because adaptive gains can grow without bound while the outputs stay bounded ([Nguyen 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140010638)). Count a run as drifting if norms grow, even when general-suite drift is flat.
- **AC5. Subspace-restricted decay (untested).** Replace the scalar decay gate with decay of the memory state restricted to recently written key directions. This will raise fact recall for topics revisited after long gaps, with no rise in general-suite drift. The idea is motivated by Goel et al.'s variable-direction forgetting. That method acts on the RLS covariance, not the state, so their result does not cover this one ([Goel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03523)). A plain delta rule without α already erases only along k. AC5 differs by shrinking stored content across the recent write subspace. If it does no better than that baseline, it is a no-op.
- **AC6. Concurrent-learning replay (untested).** Replay a few retrieval-log items with each write, as concurrent learning replays its stored data in every update ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2010](https://doi.org/10.1109/CDC.2010.5717148)). Curate the log to maximise σ_min of the stored keys, following the history-stack rule of [Chowdhary & Johnson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2011.5991481): a new point replaces an old one only if the swap raises σ_min. Editing the log on every update and deletion is our own addition. Chowdhary & Johnson 2011 assume fixed ideal weights and leave checking whether recorded data are still relevant to future work; the 2010 paper notes only that adding or removing stored points leaves its Lyapunov function unchanged (Remark 3). This will raise update accuracy and fact recall over no replay and over a recency-curated log. An unedited log will lower update accuracy, because replay re-writes stale values. A second prediction is that σ_min predicts fact recall once the log holds d_k independent keys. The exponential-rate result assumes a single fixed parameter and structured, linearly parameterised uncertainty. For RBF-NN (unstructured) uncertainty, only ultimate boundedness of the tracking and weight errors is claimed ([Chowdhary & Johnson 2011](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACC.2011.5991481)). Forget compliance is the guard metric.
- **AC7. Heuristic fallback (untested).** A simple, independently implemented monitor watches for repetition loops, divergence from the base and weight-norm bounds. When it trips:
  - output falls back to the base with the memory read-out zeroed;
  - the fast memory is reset to the last consolidated state (our addition; Neural Simplex does not reset its neural controller but retrains it online while the baseline is in control);
  - the memory path regains control only under a condition stricter than the complement of the trip condition, modelled on Neural Simplex's reverse switching ([Phan et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.00528)). Holding writes off until then is our choice: Neural Simplex retrains its controller only while the baseline is in control.

  Without a formal safe set, no Simplex-style guarantee is claimed. The fallback will cut worst-case general-suite drift. Its cost is measured as lost fact recall, and forget compliance must hold after every reset.
- **AC8. Gated consolidation (untested).** Accept a new LoRA only if replay-measured forgetting and base-capability probes do not degrade; otherwise keep the previous adapter ([Anderson 2005](https://doi.org/10.4310/cis.2005.v5.n1.a1); [Berkenkamp et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08551)). Shah et al. keep one virtual queue per past task, driven by that task's replay-buffer loss relative to a reference model, and the queue weights that task's forgetting penalty ([Shah et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08452)). A per-memory queue would be our adaptation of their per-task scheme. This borrows Berkenkamp et al.'s accept-or-revert pattern without their certificate. Compared with unconditional consolidation, it will lower general-suite drift and raise fact recall, with no loss of forget compliance.
- **AC9. Filter plus projection at a high step size (untested; expected negative).** Test the claim L1 actually makes: a low-pass filter on the memory read-out, plus projection-bounded writes at a high write step ([Cao & Hovakimyan 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0608320)), compared with projection alone. We expect the filter not to lower general-suite drift, and very high write steps to be unstable. This is by analogy with Ioannou et al., who find that the L1 filter worsens MRAC's performance and robust-margin bounds, and that very high adaptive gains make the continuous-time adaptive law stiff (risking numerical instability) and less robust to unmodelled dynamics ([Ioannou et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2014.2318871)). If the filtered arm does better, the L1 decoupling claim deserves a closer look for Engram.

## 4. Where the analogy breaks

- **Non-linear deep memories.** Most of the guarantees above are for linear estimators. The H∞ result covers LMS on a linear model with a fixed weight vector ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)). The momentum laws are continuous-time. They are proven for linear parameterisations, and for nonlinear ones only under monotonicity or convexity ([Boffi & Slotine 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13154)). The DNN concurrent-learning result rests on a Taylor expansion and gives only ultimate boundedness that depends on initial conditions ([Hart et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10678)). An MLP memory written by gradients sits mostly outside this theory. [Yang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06484) trace the delta rule to Widrow and LMS. [Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12352) identify the DeltaNet recurrence with LMS (the delta rule or Widrow–Hoff rule), and identify the δ → ∞ limit of Longhorn's adaptive step, β = 1/‖k‖², with NLMS. Tying Grazzi et al.'s (0,2) eigenvalue extension to the NLMS bound 0 < μ < 2 is our reading; Grazzi et al. do not mention LMS or NLMS.
- **No reference model and no true parameter.** MRAC measures error against a reference model and analyses the distance to θ*. Engram has neither. Its surprise is a prediction error on a text stream the model may partly generate, and a user's memory is not a fixed target vector. Control already separates the error going to zero from the parameters converging ([Åström 2018](https://lccc.control.lth.se/media/LCCC2018/WS2018-10/Slides/KJA.pdf)). For Engram, good next-token behaviour and a faithful memory likewise need separate evaluations.
- **The objectives differ.** Control can settle for safe tracking without PE, which the survey calls control for learning ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)). Engram also needs faithful recall, edits and deletion. Robust modifications trade exact tracking for boundedness ([Nguyen 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140010638)). In a memory, that residual may show up as forgetting or as biased recall. In these sources, forgetting has three roles. It is a way to track slowly varying parameters ([Sastry & Bodson 1989](https://my.ece.utah.edu/~bodson/acscr/), Sec. 5.6.4), a condition for continuing to learn ([Goel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03523)), and a price paid for robustness. As far as we found, none of the sources reviewed here treats deleting a specific item on request as a requirement.
- **The guarantees are weaker than they look.**
  - Lopes et al.'s NLMS bound is mean-only, under independence and slow-adaptation assumptions ([Lopes et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05746)).
  - For linear regression with a fixed target, deterministic results exist: the noise-free projection-algorithm bound ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336), Theorem 1, Sec. 4.5.1) and worst-case energy bounds ([Hassibi et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1109/78.485923)). The survey's deterministic results for nonlinearly parameterised systems need convexity or concavity and min-max tools ([Annaswamy & Fradkov 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.11336)).
  - Under parasitic dynamics, the modified law of Ioannou & Kokotovic, which Gaudio et al. cite for σ-modification, guarantees only a region of attraction from which signals converge to a residual set ([Ioannou & Kokotovic 1984](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.1984.1103490)).
  - Leakage gives time-averaged (mean-square-sense) bounds that still allow bursting ([Ioannou & Sun 1996](https://archive.org/details/ost-engineering-robust_adaptive_control)).
  - Nguyen, arguing for his own optimal control modification, says that for the Rohrs counterexample the σ- and e-modification parameters are chosen ad hoc and can be found only by trial and error, whereas OCM's parameter can be computed analytically ([Nguyen 2013](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20140010638)).
  - In practice, RLS performance is often extremely sensitive to the forgetting factor, and suitable values (0.99 to 0.9999) are typically found by trial and error ([Goel et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03523)).
- **Contested claims.** The L1 claim that fast adaptation is decoupled from robustness is disputed ([Ioannou et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1109/TAC.2014.2318871)), so Engram should not depend on it.
- **The inputs are not exogenous.** Convergence analyses such as [Boyd & Sastry 1986](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/papers/para_conv_adaptive.html) are stated in terms of an exogenous reference input. Engram's keys are high-dimensional, non-stationary and possibly adversarial. They are also self-generated whenever writes consume generated text. Rohrs's thesis found an infinite-gain operator in every algorithm it examined and suggested, with a hedge, that the operator is generic. It concluded that none of those algorithms could be used with confidence in practical design ([Rohrs 1982](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19830005594)). By analogy, the base model's interaction with the memory may play the role of unmodelled dynamics, and evaluations must include it.
