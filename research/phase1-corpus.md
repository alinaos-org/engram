# Phase 1 corpus: data card and build plan

*Draft, 2026-10-15. Answers [#10](https://github.com/alinaos-org/engram/issues/10) (entry gate G7 in [phase-0-plan.md](phase-0-plan.md)). Every fact about a source comes from its dataset card, licence file or paper, fetched on 2026-10-15 (see [Sources](#sources-fetched-2026-10-15)). Token counts are as each card states them, with the tokenizer named. Counts in Qwen3 tokens are **estimates** until the build script measures them. The mix, chunk sizes and costs are **assumptions**.*

## What the corpus has to do

Phase 1 trains a memory module attached to a frozen `Qwen/Qwen3-8B` base ([base-model-shortlist.md](base-model-shortlist.md); the base checkpoint `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base` shares its tokenizer). Each training **episode** writes chunks 1..k into memory, drops the context, then scores two things ([experiment-plan.md](experiment-plan.md)):

1. next-token prediction on chunk k+1;
2. answers to **probes** about chunks 1..k.

So the corpus needs long documents (many chunks per document), content whose recall can be checked, and no real personal data. Exact deletion only holds if nothing shared trains on user data (open question 11). landscape.md §8 adds a design constraint: raw next-token text alone puts knowledge into weights poorly, while varied synthetic restatements and QA work better (EntiGraph, Cartridges, Knowledge Modules). The corpus therefore pairs raw text with generated probes and restatements.

**Why each fits write-then-recall.**
- **Books (1–3)** give 100K+ token documents, so a single book yields long runs of contiguous chunks. Their characters, places and events make natural recall probes, and next-chunk prediction rewards remembering earlier plot.
- **Expository text (4–6)** gives dense facts with cross-references between sections, such as "as defined in §2". That is the closest public analogue of a user stating a fact early and relying on it later.
- **ConversationChronicles (7)** has the shape of engram-bench: five sessions with time gaps between them. Content stated in session 1 matters in session 5.
- **Synthetic biographies (8)** give exact gold answers with no LLM in the loop. They are the cleanest recall supervision, and the closest in form to engram-bench's planted facts.

**Real-thread sources (11–13).** These are the issue's "long multi-document threads", but every one is written by real, named people.

**Caveats from the cards.**
- **PG-19.** The card advises against using it "to train a general-purpose language model" because of dated style and historical bias. The base stays frozen and only the memory trains, but the style gap to conversational evals is a reason to keep a large conversational slice.
- **Common Pile.** Every card warns that "license laundering and inaccurate metadata" may mislabel some documents. Library of Congress (`common-pile/library_of_congress`, `cc-by-4.0`, 135,500 docs) is a backup books source. Its card says the licence "is not a guarantee for individual documents".

**PII and consent.**
- **Books.** Books (1–3) are public domain and old, with no living private individuals at scale.
- **arXiv and peS2o.** These contain author names and affiliations. Probes must not ask about authors (enforced in the prompt and filter).
- **Wikipedia.** It contains biographies of living people. v0 drops articles that a biography heuristic flags (lead sentence matches `born`/date patterns), and probes never target named real persons.
- **ConversationChronicles.** It is LLM-generated: "we exploit a large language model to produce the data" (EMNLP 2023 abstract; model not named). It has no real users.
- **Threads (11–13).** They hold real usernames, opinions and sometimes contact details. The Ubuntu logs are public domain, but the people in them did not consent to being in a memory model's training set. Under Engram's rule (no shared component trains on real personal data), they stay out of v0. They come back only as a scrubbed, opt-in ablation slice after a decision by Matt (see open items).

**Considered and dropped** (with the reason read on the fetched page):
- `allenai/WildChat-1M` (ODC-BY): real users' ChatGPT conversations, "alongside demographic data, including state, country, hashed IP addresses, and request headers".
- `lmsys/lmsys-chat-1m`: gated, real users, and the source of PrefEval's inter-turn filler (contamination as well as PII).
- ShareGPT and UltraChat: the LongMemEval README names them as its filler-session sources.
- `google/Synthetic-Persona-Chat` (`cc-by-4.0`): seeded from Persona-Chat, and LoCoMo samples its personas from the MSC dataset (`msc_personas_all.json`). The shared persona lineage is a contamination risk for LoCoMo.
- `storytracer/US-PD-Books` (`cc0-1.0` metadata only): full texts were removed at the Internet Archive's request.
- `allenai/peS2o` as a whole: use the Common Pile openly-licensed subset (5) instead.
- `PleIAs/common_corpus`: per-document licences, overlaps 1–6; kept as a backup.
- `deepmind/narrativeqa` (Apache-2.0): it mixes in movie scripts from imsdb, and its QA is written by MTurk workers.
- `bigcode/the-stack-github-issues`: gated, and superseded by (12).
- `google/synthbio`, `emozilla/booksum`: not found (HTTP 401).
- `institutional/institutional-books-1.0`: redirected, not assessed.

**Contamination risk with the eval sets.**
- **Low:** books, papers and Wikipedia, except arXiv. The arXiv slice can contain the benchmark papers themselves, so their ids are denylisted (below).
- **Medium:** ConversationChronicles, which has the same multi-session shape as LoCoMo and LongMemEval and may echo LLM conversational templates. It goes through the full n-gram filter.
- **Highest:** Engram's own synthetic biographies, if they reuse engram-bench's name pools or templates. The generator must be disjoint by construction (below).

## (a) v0 mix (assumption: 10B training tokens, Qwen3 tokenizer, no repeats)

The 10B total is the experiment-plan assumption. Shares are a starting point for the Phase 1 ablations, not a result. "Available" uses the Comma counts above as a sanity check that each slice is not repeated.

| Slice | Share | Tokens | Drawn from | Available (stated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Public-domain books | 35% | 3.5B | (1) 2.5B, excluding PG-19 val/test book ids; (3) 1.0B after an OCR-quality filter | 5.7B + 12.4B Comma |
| B. Long expository | 20% | 2.0B | (4) 1.0B; (5) 1.0B, full-text docs only | 6.0B + 43.3B Comma |
| C. Encyclopedic | 10% | 1.0B | (6) long articles (≥ 4K Qwen tokens, estimate), biography filter | unmeasured after filter |
| D. Synthetic biographies + QA | 15% | 1.5B | Engram bioS-style generator (below) | generated |
| E. Synthetic multi-session conversations | 15% | 1.5B | (7) all episodes (~0.2B words, derived) + Engram-generated multi-session threads (~1.2B, generated) | partly generated |
| F. Synthetic restatements | 5% | 0.5B | EntiGraph-style restatements of A/B chunks, generated; (10) only if QuALITY licences check out | generated |

Probe and answer tokens are counted inside each slice. **Cost flag.** Slices E and F plus probes for A–C come to roughly 2.5–3.5B generated tokens (assumption). At experiment-plan's assumed ~2,500 tokens/s per GPU for a 30B-class model, that is ~280–390 GPU-hours, comparable to the Phase 1 training estimate itself. Fallbacks: build 5B tokens first, use templates for most of D, and generate book probes once and reuse them across epochs.

## (b) Chunking scheme

- **Tokenizer:** the `Qwen/Qwen3-8B` tokenizer. Every count in the built data card is measured with it.
- **Write size:** each episode samples one size from {1,024, 2,048, 4,096} tokens, matching the planned 1–4K writes (LaCT, In-Place TTT). Cuts snap to the nearest paragraph boundary within ±10%, or the nearest sentence boundary. Chunk size is itself a Phase 1 ablation, so it is stored per episode.
- **Conversation sources (7, E):** one write per turn pair or per session, whichever is smaller than 4K. Session time gaps ("A few weeks") are kept as a header line.
- **Episode:** a contiguous run of k write chunks from one document. k is uniform in 1..K, where K × chunk ≤ 128K tokens, because engram-bench histories exceed a 128K window by session 30. Then come a target chunk (k+1) and probes. Documents longer than one episode are split into several episodes, each with a fresh memory.
- **Mixed episodes (20%, assumption):** chunks from 2–4 unrelated documents, separated by boundary markers. Probes ask about each, testing interference within one memory.
- **Probe lag:** probes are spread across lag buckets k−j ∈ {0, 1–3, 4–15, 16+}, so recall is measured against distance and not only on the last chunk.
- **Per-chunk record:** `source`, `doc_id`, `licence`, `char_offsets`, `sha256`, `n_tokens`, `split`. Splits are by document, never by chunk: 98% train / 1% val / 1% test (assumption). PG-19 val/test books are always held out.

## (c) Probe generation and verification

**Synthetic biographies (D), no LLM needed.**
- The design follows bioS: per person, independently sampled attributes such as birth date, city, major, university and employer, written as six sentences chosen from ~50 templates per sentence (arXiv:2309.14316).
- Engram additions, to match engram-bench's categories:
  - attribute **updates** in later chunks (moves, job changes), scored stale-value-wrong as in FAMA;
  - **abstention** probes on attributes never stated;
  - **indirect** probes ("Who should I call about the tax question?").
- Gold answers come straight from the structured record.
- **Disjoint from engram-bench:** separate name-syllable pools from `engram/bench/world.py`, separate value lists, and no shared templates. The build asserts zero overlap (see d).
- An optional LLM paraphrase pass, as in the paper's LLaMA-generated near-real variant, adds surface variety. The pass reruns the gold-string checks.

**Natural text (A, B, C, E, F).**
- **Generator:** `mistralai/Mistral-Small-3.2-24B-Instruct-2506` (`apache-2.0`, ungated per HF API).
- **Verifier:** `allenai/Olmo-3.1-32B-Instruct` (`apache-2.0`, ungated). Olmo's open training data also lets us check the verifier for contamination.
- Both are non-Qwen, per phase-0-plan decision 3. Both must also differ from whichever model writes engram-bench's LLM sessions, to avoid style leakage into the eval (eval-design open question). Record all three ids in the manifest.

**Prompt outline (generator).** The generator gets chunk j (and, for cross-chunk items, chunk j′) and returns JSON items: `{question, answer, evidence_span, type}`, with `type ∈ {fact, attribute, temporal_order, cross_chunk, abstain}`. Rules:
- answerable only from the given text, not from world knowledge;
- answer ≤ 10 words;
- `evidence_span` is a verbatim quote of ≤ 40 words;
- the question paraphrases the text rather than copying it;
- no yes/no questions;
- no questions about real living people, authors or affiliations;
- 3–5 items per chunk.

Abstain items name an entity or attribute plausibly absent from the text, with the gold answer "not stated".

**Verification (drop on any failure).**
1. `evidence_span` is an exact substring of its chunk, and the answer is contained in or entailed by the span (verifier).
2. **Open-book check:** the verifier, given the chunk, answers correctly (fuzzy match or judge).
3. **Closed-book floor:** `Qwen/Qwen3-8B-Base` without the chunk must fail. This is engram-bench's leak-verified floor, and it removes trivia about famous books.
4. Near-duplicate removal within a document (MinHash on question 5-grams).
5. **Human spot-check:** 100 probes per source before scaling, target ≥ 90% valid (assumption).

## (d) Decontamination

1. **Lineage exclusions** (whole sources, before download): ShareGPT, UltraChat (LongMemEval filler), LMSYS-Chat-1M (PrefEval inter-turn filler), and Persona-Chat/MSC-derived data (LoCoMo personas).
2. **Hold-outs:** PG-19 validation and test books are removed from (1) by Gutenberg ebook id. arXiv ids of the reused benchmark papers in eval-design.md are denylisted: 2410.10813, 2402.17753, 2502.09597, 2507.05257, 2510.27246, 2504.14225.
3. **Reference set R:**
   - engram-bench v0 generated sessions and probes, plus every template string in `engram/bench/world.py` and `writer.py`;
   - LongMemEval oracle, `_s` and `_m` (cleaned, `mit`);
   - LoCoMo `locomo10.json` (licence "Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International");
   - PrefEval `benchmark_dataset/` including `filtered_inter_turns.json` (same NC licence);
   - the other reused suites in eval-design.md, once downloaded.

   The NC licences restrict use of these sets, and evaluation data never enters training in any case.
4. **N-gram filter:** lowercase, strip punctuation, and split into words. Collect 13-word shingles from R, minus shingles that occur in >0.1% of a 1M-document background sample (stock phrases). Drop any training chunk sharing ≥ 1 remaining shingle. A dropped chunk ends its episode there.
5. **Template filter:** compile engram-bench templates into regexes with slot wildcards. Any chunk or probe that matches is dropped. Generated data (D, E) must have zero matches, or the build fails.
6. **Entity and canary sweep:** exact search for engram-bench canary strings and generated user names. Any hit fails the build. Bio names are additionally checked against engram-bench's name pools.
7. **Probe-level filter:** drop probes whose question is a near-duplicate of any eval question (MinHash Jaccard ≥ 0.8 on word 5-grams, assumption).
8. **Report:** `contamination_report.json` with per-source counts of dropped chunks and probes and the matching eval ids. It is published with the data card.

## (e) Rebuild script outline (not yet written)

```text
engram-corpus build --config configs/phase1-v0.yaml --out data/phase1/v0 --seed 0
                    [--stages fetch,filter,decontam,generate,chunk,probe,verify,pack,card]
                    [--target-tokens 10e9] [--dry-run] [--resume]

config: per source {hf_id, revision (pinned commit sha), split, licence_allowlist, share},
        generator/verifier model ids + revisions, chunk sizes, K_max, thresholds, eval paths

1 fetch     stream each source at its pinned revision; record sha, licence, card URL
2 filter    language=en; per-doc licence ∈ allowlist (PD, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, GFDL);
            length ≥ 3 chunks; OCR quality (pre-1929); biography heuristic (Wikipedia);
            MinHash dedup across sources (Gutenberg vs PG-19 overlap)
3 decontam  lineage + hold-out exclusions; 13-gram, template, canary sweeps vs R
4 generate  bio worlds (seeded); synthetic multi-session threads; restatements (vLLM)
5 chunk     tokenize (Qwen3), cut 1/2/4K at paragraph boundaries, build episodes
6 probe     templated probes (D); LLM probes (A–C, E, F)
7 verify    substring + open-book + closed-book floor + dedup; re-run probe-level decontam
8 pack      shuffle episodes by seed; write parquet shards {episode_id, writes[], target, probes[]}
9 card      measure Qwen3 token counts per source/slice/split; emit DATA_CARD.md,
            manifest.json (git commit, revisions, model ids, seeds, wall-clock), contamination_report.json
```

The script is deterministic given the seed, pinned revisions and model ids. LLM generation is cached by `(model, revision, prompt sha)`, so a rebuild re-uses completions. Stages 1–3 and 5 are testable on CPU with a tiny fixture, like `tests/fixtures/`.
